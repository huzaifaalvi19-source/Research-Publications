# Research-Publications
Proportional Analysis and Microstructural Characterization of Threading Dies Manufactured Using Conventional and EDM Machining Methods
<br>
## **Research Overview**
<br>
This research investigates the manufacturing and performance of threading dies fabricated from H13 die steel using two different machining approaches: conventional machining and Electrical Discharge Machining (EDM). The study was conducted to understand how the machining method affects the material properties, microstructure, dimensional performance, operational life, and failure behavior of threading dies.
<br>
The experimental work compares the effects of mechanically induced stresses from conventional machining with the non-contact material removal process of EDM. The fabricated dies were evaluated through hardness testing, microstructural characterization, and threading performance experiments to determine differences in their structural stability and service life.
<br>
The results showed that conventional machining produced a significant increase in die hardness, which was associated with higher mechanically induced stresses and earlier failure. In comparison, the EDM-fabricated dies exhibited minimal variation in hardness and better structural integrity. Microstructural analysis further indicated greater grain structure stability in the EDM-processed dies.
<br>
Performance testing demonstrated a measurable difference in service life. The conventionally machined threading die failed after threading 16 inches of mild steel rods, whereas the EDM-fabricated die successfully threaded up to 20 inches under the experimental conditions.
<br>
Overall, the study demonstrates the influence of machining processes on the microstructure, mechanical behavior, dimensional performance, and durability of H13 threading dies. The findings provide insight into the potential advantages of EDM for manufacturing tooling where reduced machining-induced stresses, structural stability, and improved service life are important considerations.
